What it does and doesn’t do

Where RouteFlow stops.

RouteFlow runs the selling side, from order to payment. It also does buying and simple stock. It doesn’t keep your books.

What it does.

  • SellOrders, prices, promotions, and estimates.
  • Let stores orderStores see their own prices and order online.
  • Get paidBills, payments, and returns.
  • Buy and keep stockSuppliers and what you have.
  • DeliverRoutes and a driver app.
  • Sell at the counterScan items and print a receipt.
  • See your numbersSales, profit, and who owes you.
  • Sell regulated itemsGoods like tobacco, with extra legal rules.

The details

What it doesn’t do.

A plain list, so you can check it against what you need.

What isn’t in RouteFlow?

Not in RouteFlowWhat you get instead
Separate warehousesEach product has one count. There are no stock locations.
A general ledgerNo chart of accounts, journals, balance sheet, or period close.
FIFO or LIFO costingCost is a weighted average.
Lots, expiry dates, and binsNot tracked.
EDIRouteFlow doesn’t connect by EDI.
Accounting toolsNo connection to QuickBooks, Xero, or Zoho. Move data with CSV files.
CarriersNo connection to a carrier. An order can keep the carrier’s name.
A live map of your driversOn the web, you see each stop. You don’t see the driver’s truck on a map.
Text messagesNotices go by email.
Two-step login, single sign-on, an API, or webhooks you set upNone of these are in RouteFlow.

Where are the books?

Reports come from your sales and expenses. RouteFlow also tracks expenses, receipts, and mileage.

Keep your accountant and your accounting tool.

How does data move?

You import files, and you can export your customers, products, and stock history.

It’s all CSV files, and you do the move.

How is stock kept?

Each product has one count. Cost is a weighted average.

You can buy from suppliers, receive the goods, and count stock.

When is it the wrong choice?

RouteFlow isn’t right for you if you need:

  • Several warehouses
  • EDI customers
  • A general ledger inside the tool
  • Lot or expiry tracking

A spreadsheet, a big business system, or this.

This is how we see the three. It’s our own view, not research.

  • A spreadsheet

    It’s free to start, and you know it already. By itself, it won’t give each store its price, plan a route, or take an order from a store.

  • A big business system

    It often covers books, warehouses, and EDI. It’s usually bigger, and it takes longer to start.

  • RouteFlow

    It covers the selling side, from order to payment. The trial is 14 days, with no card. It doesn’t do the things in the list above.

Words we use.

A few terms, in plain words.

  • Regulated items. Goods like tobacco have extra legal rules. We turn this on for you.
  • Price level. A group of stores that share a price.
  • Box and piece. You can sell by the box, by the piece, or both.
  • Workspace ID. The name you type to sign in.
